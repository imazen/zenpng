# APNG encode throughput vs pre-streaming main (2026-10-15)

Frame-by-frame APNG streaming (PR 45) left RGB8 animation slower than the
pre-PR main at some efforts and RGBA8 slower at most: every pushed frame was
converted to RGBA8 (two canvas-sized conversions for RGB8 input), the dispose
/blend lookahead re-walked each delta region several times, and every trial
compression materialized and then discarded its output bytes.

This change keeps accumulated frames in the pushed layout (RGB8 stays RGB8),
fuses the RGBA8 eligibility scan with OVER-subframe construction, reuses the
winning lookahead's subframes as the next frame's own evaluation, and cuts
allocation churn with thread-local scratch pools plus a size-only compression
path for trials (only the length decides dispose/blend).

Output is byte-identical to the previous tree on the oracle
(`tests/apng_byte_identity.rs`, RGB8/RGBA8 ±transparency, 16-bit, efforts
0/1/2/5/7/default, downcast on/off, with and without `begin`) and on the
benchmark's own comparisons (`old` vs `main` vs `new` vs `new+begin` all match
`main` byte counts below).

## Setup

- Workload: 8 frames of a 320x240 window into a photo
  (`STREAMING_REGRESS_PHOTO`, shifted per frame), pushed through the zencodec
  animation API; `DowncastFlags::none()`, `ThreadingPolicy::Sequential`.
- `old` = zenpng 52acca5a (pre-PR-45); `main` = zenpng eca68431 (post-PR-45);
  `new` = this work; `new+begin` = same, streamed via `begin`+`take_output`.
- zenbench interleaved groups, `taskset -c 2-7 --cpu-policy=unrestricted`
  (dev's asymmetric L3 refuses unpinned runs); iterate mode, 30 rounds.
- Harness: the `apng-harness` scratch crate (same protocol as
  `zencodec/perf/streaming-regress`).

Instruction/cycle spot check, rgba8 e7 (`perf stat`, (12−4)/8 per iter):
new 1.359B instr / 409.8M cycles vs old 1.429B instr / 421.6M cycles
(−4.9% / −2.8%).

## rgba8 default is structurally bounded

`old` emitted RGB (colortype 2) for opaque RGBA8 input — it ignored
`DowncastFlags::none()` — so its e13 pipeline deflated 3 bytes/px. Byte
identity with `main` pins `new` to RGBA output (colortype 6): 200,862 vs
184,890 output bytes, and ~4/3 the deflate input. A perf record of `new` at
e13 attributes 94.5% of samples to zenflate deflate (~551 ms of the 583 ms
encode, on the mandated screen+refine+fallback passes); `old`'s *entire*
encode is 376.8 ms. No allocation or evaluation change can close that — only a
faster zenflate (out of scope) or different output bytes (forbidden) could.
Every other case beats `old`, and `new` is ~11% faster than `main` here.
